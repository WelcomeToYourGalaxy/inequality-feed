What this is. A feed on economic inequality worldwide, in fourteen subjects. It opens where the section it follows opens — with self-sufficiency, and the fact that the freedom not to take part in a system requires the means to live outside it — then covers the facets that section lists: wages and executive pay, wealth and inheritance, social mobility, purchasing power and basic necessities, access to credit, regional inequality, discrimination in the labour market, access to public services, taxation, and unequal access to lawmakers. Nothing here is written or summarised: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

The market, and how much of it is here. The section calls the market the most powerful driver of inequality, and three subjects carry that: who owns the market, because the section's point is that a tiny group concentrates the wealth everyone else produces; the ladder pulled up, because a field made by experts for experts, priced in research, technology and time, is one most people cannot enter; and private markets and who is allowed in, because the law reserves the best of it for the already-rich through accredited-investor thresholds while public listings shrink.

What it is not. Market machinery on its own is not this subject. Trading plumbing, enforcement actions, systemic-risk warnings and tax mechanics — front running, dark pools, insider dealing cases, derivatives clearing, stress tests, buybacks and carried interest — are refused unless a story ties them to who ends up with what. Nor is the daily ticker: shares rose, earnings beats, price targets, analyst upgrades, best-stocks-to-buy, and the sponsored content around them. Every subject term is gated behind the words it must appear beside.

Standing. Every wire is labelled by what kind of source it is, and every row carries the label. Regulators, central banks & institutions is the bodies that set or supervise the rules — the IMF, the World Bank, the OECD, the BIS, the Financial Stability Board, central banks, securities regulators and courts. Research & inequality data is the institutes and the statistical work. Campaigning & accountability groups is the organisations arguing with all of it — tax justice, debt justice, corporate accountability and transparency groups. Press is general news across 26 languages. Standing is provenance, not a claim that a story is important.

Weight. Separately, each story is scored on what it carries: a decision — a ruling, a law enacted, a fine, a settlement, a merger blocked, a bailout (2); institutional material — a central bank or regulator publication, an IMF or OECD report, official statistics, a peer-reviewed study (2); a measured figure such as a share of wealth, a Gini coefficient, a notional value or assets under management (1); a pending decision with a date attached (1); a named jurisdiction (1); and a primary source (1). At three or more the row is marked consequential, and the Weight filter narrows to those. A campaign statement and a central bank stability warning both appear here; the pips are how you tell them apart at a glance, and the words beside them say what earned the score.

What is refused. The daily ticker and the tip sheet, the market machinery described above, sponsored content, trading courses and passive-income promotion, and entertainment that uses the vocabulary. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.
